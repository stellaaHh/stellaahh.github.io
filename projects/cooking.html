01

HusCooks

Making shared cooking easier to coordinate.

HusCooks is a cooking coordination app for UW students living with roommates. Our research explored why students who wanted to cook together often found it easier to cook alone, leading us to develop a prototype that helps them turn a conversation about a shared meal into a plan.

Role
UX Research & Product Design
Timeline
September 2025 – March 2026
Type
UX Research & Digital Product Design
Methods
Interviews · Research Synthesis · Figma Prototyping · Usability Testing

My contribution

I contributed to interviews and research synthesis before working with the team on prototyping and testing. I illustrated the Cooking Coordination App storyboard and designed the Reflection interface, with broader product decisions developed together as a team.

02

Why Cooking Together Is Hard

We conducted semi-structured interviews with four UW undergraduates living off campus with roommates to understand their cooking routines and experiences preparing meals together. Although participants valued shared cooking, the effort of coordinating a meal often made cooking alone the more convenient choice.

Research findings

Finding a shared time

Participants described difficulty coordinating their schedules, which made finding a time to cook together a central requirement for the design.

Keeping planning manageable

Because cooking alone often felt simpler than organizing a group meal, we needed to consider whether each proposed feature reduced the planning effort or added another task.

Making room for the social experience

Participants valued cooking as a way to spend time with roommates, so our concepts considered how to help them begin a shared meal and revisit the experience afterward.

Design question

How might we help roommates plan and coordinate shared meals without adding more work to their routines?

03

Exploring Solutions

We drew storyboards separately before comparing three approaches: a cooking coordination app, a physical mood board, and an in-person cooking class. Looking at the scenarios together helped us consider how each approach would fit into roommates’ lives before developing the app concept further.

04

Designing Through Feedback

We developed our ideas through low-fidelity sketches and a mid-fidelity Figma prototype, then used concept testing, usability testing, and expert feedback to guide the high-fidelity design.

Two changes became especially important.

From Chat to Scheduling

Our mid-fidelity prototype included Chat as a place to discuss cooking plans. Although usability testing and an expert review suggested making it easier to find, we also recognized that roommates already used other messaging apps and might have little reason to move those conversations.

We therefore replaced Chat with Group Scheduling in the high-fidelity prototype, giving roommates a place to view upcoming sessions and check who was participating after discussing the meal elsewhere.

My contribution

Redesigning Reflection

I designed Reflection to help roommates revisit their cooking experiences, initially using a line chart to show mood history. Concept testing revealed that this view was unclear, and later feedback suggested using emojis.

In the final prototype, I connected each reflection to food photos, dates, and emoji mood indicators so readers could refer back to a particular meal. Working on the revision made me reconsider what the page needed to help people remember, since a chart of change over time offered a different kind of information from a photo and mood attached to one evening.

05

Final Experience & Reflection

The final prototype follows a short path: Home to find a meal, Groups to coordinate with roommates, and Reflection afterward.

The project ended at the prototype stage. Testing helped us improve the interface and reconsider the feature set, but we did not measure whether HusCooks changed roommates' cooking habits over time.

What I Learned

HusCooks taught me to consider a feature within the routine it would enter. Chat seemed useful when we looked at the app on its own, but roommates already had ways to communicate, and our design needed to account for that. In Reflection, seeing others struggle with a chart I understood well reminded me how much familiarity could shape my judgment.

If I continued the project, I would follow roommate groups over several cooking sessions to observe whether they returned to Groups to check their plans and found a reason to revisit Reflection afterward. Their use over time would help us decide which parts of the app were worth developing and which still asked too much of them.
